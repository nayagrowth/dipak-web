When I went to 8th standard, I was extremely happy.

Because I thought:

“Finally! Ab koi mujhe speech ke liye force nahi karega.” 😂

I studied in a government school.

And yes, I was a bright student.

Not the “my mother says I'm bright” kind.

Actually bright. 😄

I was pretty good at almost everything.

Studies.

Competitions.

Activities.

Whatever teachers asked me to do, I could usually manage.

Except one thing.

Public speaking.

That was my kryptonite.

Every 15th August.

Every 26th January.

Somehow my teachers would look at me and think:

> “Haan, isko stage pe bhejte hain.”

And I would be thinking:

“Madam, please. I am begging you.” 😂


### The funny thing was...

I wasn't afraid of the speech.

I was afraid of what would happen to me once I reached the stage.

I could prepare the entire speech.

Read it ten times.

Memorize every line.

Practice it in my head.

And then I'd walk onto the stage...

My heart would start racing.

My hands would feel different.

My brain would suddenly decide:

> “You know what? I think I'm taking the day off.”

😂

I'd remember the first few lines.

Then...

blank.

Gone.

The same brain that had remembered the entire speech five minutes ago suddenly couldn't remember what came after:

> “Respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends...”

And honestly, that famous statement:

> “People fear public speaking more than death.”

Sometimes I think whoever came up with that statement must have borrowed the data from my childhood. 😂


## But something changed later.

I didn't suddenly become confident.

I practiced.

That's an important distinction.

I started with Zoom sessions.

One-to-one became one-to-many.

I started speaking to my team.

Okay...

Initially, that was basically me giving them lectures. 😂

Then I started training my in-house sales team.

Then I started speaking in colleges.

Then more training sessions.

More audiences.

More stages.

More conversations.

And slowly...

the thing I once wanted to escape...

became something I genuinely enjoy.

Today, I can stand in front of a room full of people and speak comfortably.

Not because I was born with some magical public-speaking gene.

Because I put in the repetitions.


### And that's probably the first thing I want to tell anyone who is afraid of public speaking.

You don't become a good speaker by waiting until you feel confident.

You become confident because you keep speaking.

That's the part nobody tells you.

People think confidence comes first.

Then you speak.

Actually, very often...

speaking comes first.

Then confidence follows.

It's like going to the gym.

You don't wait for your muscles to become strong before lifting weights.

You lift weights...

and your muscles become strong.

Public speaking works the same way.

The stage is your gym.


## So who should learn public speaking?

Honestly?

Almost everyone.

But if you're in business...

I'd put it extremely high on the list.

For me, after sales...

public speaking is probably the #2 skill you should develop.

And there's a very simple reason.

Leverage.


### Let me show you what I mean.

Imagine you're a salesperson.

You spend an hour with one prospect.

You understand their problem.

You build trust.

You explain your solution.

And they buy.

Let's say that deal generates ₹1 lakh.

Good.

Now imagine you have the ability to stand in front of 100 relevant people...

and communicate the same core idea in that same hour.

Let's say 20 of them become customers.

Suddenly:

20 × ₹1 lakh = ₹20 lakh.

Same hour.

Different leverage.

Of course, real life isn't that neat.

Not everyone in the room will be your ideal customer.

Not everyone will buy.

And public speaking doesn't magically turn ₹1 lakh into ₹20 lakh.

But that's not the point.

The point is:

Your time doesn't have to be limited to one conversation at a time.

That's the power of public speaking.


## And this is where public speaking connects beautifully with personal branding.

Think about people like Steve Jobs.

Elon Musk.

Barack Obama.

And many successful political leaders in India.

You may agree with everything they say.

You may disagree with everything they say.

That's not the point.

Watch what happens when they stand in front of thousands or millions of people.

One person speaks.

Millions listen.

That's leverage.

A salesperson has to repeatedly explain the same idea to individual people.

A powerful speaker can communicate an idea to thousands of people at once.

And when you consistently do that...

something else starts happening.

People begin associating you with that idea.

That's personal branding.


### This is why I don't see public speaking as just a “communication skill.”

I see it as a distribution skill.

You have an idea in your head.

Normally, you can transfer it to one person.

Maybe five.

Maybe ten.

But public speaking allows you to take that same idea...

and distribute it to hundreds or thousands of people simultaneously.

Your knowledge becomes scalable.

Your experience becomes scalable.

Your story becomes scalable.

Your point of view becomes scalable.

You stop selling only with your time.

You start selling with your ability to communicate at scale.


### And here's something else I've noticed.

People don't remember every sentence you say.

They remember how you made them feel and what you made them think about.

That's why I don't believe great public speaking is about memorizing impressive lines.

It's about creating moments.

A question that makes people think.

A story that makes them laugh.

An example that makes them say:

> “That's exactly what happens!”

A pause before an important point.

A statement that challenges something they believed for years.

That's what makes a speech memorable.


### And this is where I think many speakers go wrong.

They try to sound like speakers.

Suddenly their voice changes.

Their vocabulary becomes complicated.

Every sentence sounds rehearsed.

They start using words they would never use in a normal conversation.

And the audience can feel it.

I don't want to sound like a speaker.

I want to sound like me... who happens to be speaking to a lot of people.

There's a difference.

The moment you stop performing “public speaking”...

and start having a conversation with a room...

something changes.


## I also love studying public speaking.

Two books that influenced the way I look at it are Dale Carnegie's The Art of Public Speaking and Carmine Gallo's Talk Like TED.

What I love about books like these isn't simply learning how to stand on a stage.

It's understanding why some ideas travel further than others.

Because ultimately...

public speaking isn't really about the stage.

The stage is just the place where the skill becomes visible.

The real skill is:

Can you take something inside your head and make another person see it inside theirs?

That's communication at a much higher level.


### And there's one more reason I think salespeople should learn it.

Imagine you're a salesperson who is excellent one-to-one...

but uncomfortable in front of 20 people.

You've mastered persuasion at the smallest level.

But you've left an enormous amount of leverage on the table.

Now imagine you can do both.

You can sit across from one decision-maker...

and

you can stand in front of 500 potential buyers.

You can close individually...

and

you can create demand collectively.

You can have sales conversations...

and

you can build an audience.

That's a completely different level of leverage.


### And maybe that's why my relationship with public speaking is a little funny.

The thing I was desperately trying to avoid in school...

is now one of the things I genuinely enjoy doing.

The boy who used to stand on stage and forget his speech...

now trains sales teams.

Speaks in colleges.

Runs sessions.

Talks to rooms full of people.

And spends his time teaching other people how to communicate and sell.

Funny how life works. 😄

So if you're someone who says:

> “I'm just not a public speaker.”

I'd challenge that.

Maybe you're not a public speaker yet.

Maybe you simply haven't done enough repetitions.

Because public speaking isn't a talent reserved for people who were born confident.

It's a skill.

And like every skill...

you can train it.

And once you do...

you discover something very powerful:

You are no longer limited to convincing one person at a time.

That's when public speaking stops being a soft skill...

and starts becoming leverage.
